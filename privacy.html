Privacy policy

Last updated: 3 November 2026

Astra is a private home assistant used by one household on its own tablet. This policy explains what Astra stores, where, and how to remove it. Astra has no advertising, no analytics or tracking, and never sells data. It sends information to the services listed under Other services only to do what the household asks.

What Astra stores

Astra's server runs on Fly.io in Sydney, Australia. On that server Astra stores:

  • Household settings the household enters: playback volume, monthly budget, and household members' names, roles and notes.
  • Approximate home location: the tablet shares its location with the server so Astra can show local weather and prepare the morning routine. The tablet rounds the position to about 1 km before it is sent (two decimal places of latitude and longitude), so the server never receives or stores an exact position or street address. Astra also stores the tablet's time zone, when the location was last updated, and the name of the suburb or town that position falls in (shown on the weather card). Astra asks Android for approximate location only, never precise or background location, and the tablet checks when the app opens, when it is used again, and every few hours while it is open.
  • Reminders and routines the household creates.
  • Usage totals for running costs: numbers of tokens, conversations, conversation durations and response times.
  • Astra's memory of the household: short facts the household asks Astra to remember, or that Astra picks up from conversations, such as a pet's name, allergies and health notes, birthdays and anniversaries, preferences, standing instructions about how to treat you, and films and shows you have already watched. Each fact is kept until it is deleted. Astra never keeps passwords, PINs, card or bank numbers, ID numbers or one-time codes, even if asked. Astra also keeps a short private note about how each person likes to be spoken to, written from conversations.
  • The text of conversations (never the audio): what was said and what Astra answered, kept for up to 90 days so Astra can recall what you talked about earlier in the day and work out what is worth remembering each night. Anything that looks like a password, PIN, card or ID number is removed before the text is saved. Saving can be paused and all conversations deleted at any time in Settings.
  • A private log of the screens Astra makes: the words of the request and which screen was shown, kept for up to 90 days, to check that Astra is working well. It never holds keys or web search text, and it can be viewed and cleared in Settings.
  • The shopping list the household builds.
  • Paired devices: a name and a one-way hash of each tablet's access key.
  • Google authorisation (only if Google Calendar is connected): the Google refresh token, encrypted with AES-256-GCM. The encryption key is kept separately as a server secret.
  • Spotify authorisation (only if Spotify is connected): the Spotify refresh token, encrypted the same way. Disconnecting Spotify in Astra’s Settings deletes it.

The tablet stores its own access key in Android's encrypted secure storage.

What Astra does not store

  • Voice audio. During a conversation, audio travels directly from the tablet to OpenAI's Realtime API, and spoken replies come back the same way. Astra does not record or keep audio. The words that are spoken are saved only as text, as described above. OpenAI processes the audio under its privacy policy and API data usage policies.
  • Calendar events. Events stay in Google Calendar. Astra reads them from Google when it needs to show or discuss them, keeps only a temporary in-memory cache while its server is running, and never saves copies to disk.

Google user data

When the household connects a Google account, Astra asks for two permissions:

  • View and edit events on your calendars (calendar.events): to show events and to create, change or delete events when a household member asks.
  • See the list of your calendars (calendar.calendarlist.readonly): to know which calendars exist and which ones can be edited.

Google data is used only to provide these calendar features to the household. It is not used for advertising, not sold, not used to train AI models, and not shared with anyone. When a household member asks Astra about their calendar by voice, the relevant event details are passed to the OpenAI conversation so Astra can answer, and are not stored afterwards.

Astra's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Other services

  • Voice conversations (OpenAI): as well as your voice, the start of each conversation gives OpenAI the facts in Astra's memory (including allergies, health notes and birthdays), what was said earlier that day, and where home is (your suburb, or the home address if you entered one), so Astra can answer properly. Details needed to answer a question, such as calendar events, are passed along for that answer only.
  • Web search (OpenAI): when you ask something that needs the web, your question is sent to OpenAI's web search.
  • Weather (Google, with Open-Meteo as a backup): the approximate (1 km) home coordinates are sent to the Google Maps Platform Weather API to get the forecast. If Google is not available, they go to Open-Meteo instead. The coordinates of a city you ask about, and city search, use Open-Meteo's GeoNames-based geocoding.
  • Directions and maps (Google): when you ask for directions, how far somewhere is, or when to leave, the place you ask about and your home (the approximate saved location, or the home address if you have entered one) are sent to Google's Geocoding and Routes services. The map shown on screen is loaded from Google Maps, so Google sees that the map was requested. If Google is not available, OpenStreetMap routing is used instead.
  • Suburb name: when the saved home changes, the server sends the same approximate (1 km) coordinates, and nothing else about you or the household, to Nominatim, the free reverse-geocoding service of OpenStreetMap, to find the suburb or town name. If you enter a home address in Settings, the address text is sent once to Nominatim to find its place on the map. Like any web request, it also reaches the service from the server's network address, which is Fly.io's, not the tablet's. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).
  • Screen design (Anthropic): when you ask Astra for something to show on screen, the server sends the text of your request, a short note from the conversation, the current date and time, your home suburb name, and a few notes from Astra's memory that help the screen suit the household (allergies, food preferences, films already watched) to Anthropic, whose Claude model designs the screen. Nothing else about you or the household is sent. A copy of the request is kept in Astra's private screen log (see above), and Anthropic handles it under its own privacy policy and API terms.
  • Remembering (Anthropic): once a night, at about 2 am, the server sends that day's conversation text and Astra's current memory to Anthropic, whose Claude model decides which facts are worth keeping for good. Passwords, PINs, card, bank and ID numbers are removed first. Anthropic handles it under its own privacy policy and API terms.
  • Films and TV (TMDB): the titles of films and shows you ask about are sent to TMDB to show posters, cast photos and details.
  • Lyrics (LRCLIB): to show lyrics, the server sends the song name, artist, album and length to LRCLIB, a free lyrics database. Nothing else is sent.
  • Photos and places: to show a photo, the name of the person, place, landmark, film or dish you asked about is sent to Wikipedia, and the photo is loaded from Wikimedia Commons. To show a place on a map, the place name is sent to Nominatim (OpenStreetMap) and the map pictures are loaded from OpenStreetMap.
  • Music (Spotify): if you connect Spotify, the server uses your Spotify account to search for songs and to show what you recently played, your playlists and library. What you ask Astra to play is sent to Spotify as a search. Spotify’s app on the tablet does the playing, and Astra does not receive your Spotify password. See Spotify’s privacy policy.
  • Hosting: Fly.io hosts Astra's server and its storage, with automatic daily volume snapshots kept for a few days.

Disconnecting and deleting data

  • Disconnect Google Calendar with Disconnect in Astra's Settings under Google Calendar. Astra asks Google to revoke its access and deletes the stored Google authorisation. You can also remove Astra at any time from your Google Account under Third-party apps & services.
  • Household details, reminders and routines can be edited or deleted in Astra, or by asking Astra.
  • Memory and conversations: in Astra's Settings under Memory you can forget any single fact, forget everything, pause saving conversations, and delete all saved conversations. You can also ask Astra to forget something. The screen log can be cleared in Settings under System.
  • Location: turn location off for Astra in Android's settings and the tablet stops sharing it; the last saved approximate location stays on the server until it is replaced or removed by the developer on request.
  • Everything else, including usage totals, the shopping list and paired devices, can be removed by the developer on request. Deleted data also disappears from the automatic snapshots once they expire.
